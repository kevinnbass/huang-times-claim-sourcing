Reading it: a square is one attribution, so a source cited five times for a claim is five squares; the figure shows how much of the text about a claim rests on each kind of source, not how many people there are. "With nobody on the record" adds three kinds: people the article does not name; a conclusion the article ascribes to an agency without quoting anyone for it ("U.S. intelligence determined"); and intelligence or a memo whose contents the article relays without saying the paper saw it. A denial is drawn under the claim it denies, in the disputing column. For the first four claims, which can be stated or denied, a source counts as stating the claim only if it is itself reported as saying, concluding or showing that the thing is so; a general remark, a reaction to the reports, or a weaker statement is drawn in the next column and counted for nobody. For the relationship that column holds the lines reporting closeness or contact (a "close relationship", photographs together, being in contact) without saying it was romantic or intimate. How it was made: the 345 attributions are those the first reading found in the eleven reports, each matched to the article by script. Two further reader lanes (a different model family from the one that drew this) each filed every line under one of seventeen claims, with a stance and a basis. The list of claims was written for this figure before the filing; another list would cut the lines differently. The two readers agree on the claim for 332 of 345 lines, on the stance for 344 and on the basis for 334. A third pass by two more readers put the stating-or-commenting question to all 79 lines filed as saying so under the first four claims, named and unnamed alike; they agree on 78, and both find that 24 do not state the claim. A line is counted under a value only where both readers agree; the rest is drawn grey. In the place-by-place list, the purple bar is a script rule, not a reader's judgment: the sentence holding the attribution contains "The Times reported" or the like, or the line is itself such a citation; "names a U.S. agency" is a search of each place for agency names. The four passages from the first report were chosen for this figure. Across all claims, 90 of the 251 attributions stating a claim or giving the information have nobody on the record. What the record does not show: who any unnamed source is, what documents the paper holds but does not describe, or whether any claim is true. A claim attributed only to unnamed sources is not thereby false, and one on the record is not thereby true. Reporting on classified intelligence commonly rests on unnamed sources. No other paper's reports were counted, so nothing here says whether this pattern is unusual. Limits: eleven articles saved by hand, one paper; the readers' filings have not been audited.
